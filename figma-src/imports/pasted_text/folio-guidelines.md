Follow the existing Folio Guidelines.

Refine the existing Folio design rather than redesigning it.

Preserve the current horizontal scrolling, snapping, scene transitions, floating movement, atmospheric backgrounds, tilted project imagery, project palettes, and overall spatial personality. These elements are working well.

The goal of this pass is to combine the creativity of Folio with the clarity and information hierarchy of an excellent professional resume.

INTRO / HERO

Redesign the opening hero.

The current hero is too abstract and does not communicate enough about the person behind the work.

Treat the opening scene conceptually like the top section of an excellent resume—but interpreted through the Folio visual language.

Include:

Kevin Stahl

Software Developer · Digital Commerce · Systems

A short 1–2 sentence professional summary describing a multidisciplinary builder who designs and develops digital products, commerce experiences, and technical systems.

Include a small professional portrait/image area integrated into the composition. It should not dominate the screen. Treat it like a modern editorial resume portrait: personal enough to establish identity, but secondary to the name and professional positioning.

Also include immediately readable actions or information for:

Projects · Experience · Skills · Contact / Resume

Do not turn this into a conventional centered hero.

Keep the spatial, floating Folio environment and create an editorial composition with strong left-to-right movement toward the first project.

The visitor should understand within approximately five seconds:

Who Kevin is → what he does → what kind of work he has built → where to explore next.

MIND MAP — MORE ORGANIZED, NOT LESS INTERESTING

Preserve the collapsible mind-map concept. It is a core part of Folio and should remain visually playful, floating, connected, and subtly animated.

However, make its default layout more regular, intentional, and immediately scannable.

Reduce excessive random spacing and arbitrary positioning.

Arrange first-level nodes using a recognizable spatial rhythm around the project. They do not need to form a perfect grid or perfect circle, but their placement should feel deliberately organized.

Think:

structured constellation rather than scattered constellation.

Maintain slight asymmetry, floating motion, small rotations, depth, curved connections, and organic character so it does not become a corporate flowchart.

INFORMATION PRIORITY

Make first-level nodes communicate big ideas before details.

Prioritize categories such as:

What I Built
Why / Problem
My Role
Skills Used
Technical Build
Results
Lessons Learned

Technology stacks should remain accessible, but they should not visually overpower the project's purpose, contribution, results, or lessons.

Each major node should contain a prominent title and useful one-line preview.

Example:

WHAT I BUILT
Dynamic formula platform powered by symbolic computation.

TECHNICAL BUILD
Laravel · Livewire · MySQL · SymPy

LESSONS LEARNED
Designing reusable systems instead of one-off calculators.

Increase the size and contrast of this supporting text. The visitor should gain useful information without needing to click every node.

EXPAND ON CLICK

Begin implementing clear expandable states for the mind map.

Clicking a first-level node should reveal a small number of second-level concepts while preserving the surrounding spatial context.

Example:

TECHNICAL BUILD

expands into:

Architecture
Formula Engine
Data Model
Frontend

Selecting Architecture may then reveal a larger detail panel or deeper branch.

Use progressive disclosure:

Project → Big Idea → Supporting Concepts → Deep Detail

Do not expose the entire tree simultaneously.

When one branch expands:

move nearby objects smoothly to create space
strengthen the active connector
slightly emphasize the selected node
subtly de-emphasize unrelated branches
preserve enough surrounding context that the visitor never feels lost

Expanded information should be significantly easier to read than the ambient map.

VISUAL STYLE — SOFT BRUTALISM + COSMIC FOLIO

Introduce a small amount of playful brutalist graphic language into the existing Folio aesthetic.

Experiment selectively with:

offset hard shadows
crisp dark outlines
slightly chunky interactive objects
unexpected borders
deliberately imperfect alignment
occasional bold flat-color surfaces
strong typographic hierarchy

Combine these with the existing:

nebula atmosphere
soft gradients and light
floating bubbles
tilted imagery
depth
gentle movement
orbital connections

The contrast is intentional:

soft atmospheric world + crisp tactile interface objects.

Do not turn the entire website into pure neo-brutalism.

Brutalist characteristics should primarily help interactive objects pop from the atmospheric background and improve readability.

MOVEMENT

Preserve the existing subtle floating and movement.

More organized does not mean static.

Nodes may gently bob, drift a few pixels, breathe, tilt slightly, or respond subtly to the pointer while remaining anchored to an understandable base position.

The underlying layout should be organized.

The animation creates life around that organization.

Structure is stable. Motion is playful.

DO NOT REMOVE

Preserve:

horizontal travel
scene snapping
floating movement
tilted/background project imagery
atmospheric project-specific color
depth and layering
connected mind-map relationships
playful interaction
persistent journey navigation

Do not simplify Folio into a conventional portfolio or resume webpage.

TARGET

The result should feel like:

an exceptionally readable interactive resume that escaped the page and became a living spatial world.

A recruiter should be able to scan it quickly.

A developer should be able to explore it deeply.

And somebody casually visiting should want to play with it.
